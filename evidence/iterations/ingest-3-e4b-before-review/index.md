## Concepts

- [[Counterculture Ethos]]: The counterculture ethos of the 1960s merged with technology, leading to a philosophy of personal liberation through computing.
- [[Five Pillars]]: The five pillars are the foundational elements that have shaped and maintained Silicon Valley's competitive edge over the long term.
- [[Legal System]]: California's legal framework supports tech innovation by allowing firms to take equity and by prohibiting non-compete agreements.
- [[Silicon Valley Emergence]]: Silicon Valley emerged from an agricultural area, evolving into a federally funded research and defense economy driven by wartime and Cold War investment.
- [[Venture Capital]]: Venture capital is a form of investment that finances high-risk startups by taking equity and absorbing potential losses, which was initially supported by government programs.

## Course Logistics

- [[Founder Interview Process]]: The founder interview is a graded checkpoint where the entire team must participate in a 30–60 minute discussion with the startup's founder or a tech professional.
- [[Founder Outreach Strategy]]: Students are advised to begin outreach immediately due to the time founders take to respond, and they should use professional methods to contact founders.
- [[Immigration in Silicon Valley]]: Immigrants have historically been drawn to Silicon Valley for education, subsequently becoming company founders.
- [[Midterm Exam Logistics]]: The Midterm Exam is scheduled for Thursday 10/1 during lecture and will be open note and open book.
- [[Professional Profile Building]]: Building a professional profile involves gaining experience through various activities that provide evidence and connections.
- [[Regional Models]]: The course compares the networked Silicon Valley model with the more insular Route 128 model.
- [[Silicon Valley Themes]]: The course explores several major themes related to Silicon Valley, including immigration, the role of the state, and regional models.
- [[Startup Research Resources]]: Several online platforms are provided to help students research and verify the details of potential startups.
- [[Startup Selection Rules]]: Startups must meet specific criteria to be eligible for the competition, including being pre-Series A, founded in Silicon Valley, and no more than three years old.
- [[Team Start-Up Competition]]: The Team Start-Up Competition requires students to adopt a real early-stage startup, interview its founder, and pitch it at Demo Day, accounting for 20% of the course grade.
