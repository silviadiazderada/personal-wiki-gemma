# Week 2 Section

This source provides a comprehensive review of the history of Silicon Valley, detailing the five key pillars that fostered its growth and the unique cultural threads that have run through its development. It covers the role of government funding, research universities, venture capital, immigration, and the legal framework in shaping the region's economic landscape.

## Topics covered
- [[Counterculture Ethos]]: The counterculture ethos of the 1960s merged with technology, leading to a philosophy of personal liberation through computing.
- [[Five Pillars]]: The five pillars are the foundational elements that have shaped and maintained Silicon Valley's competitive edge over the long term.
- [[Key Figures]]: Several individuals were instrumental in shaping the technological and institutional foundations of Silicon Valley.
- [[Legal System]]: California's legal framework supports tech innovation by allowing firms to take equity and by prohibiting non-compete agreements.
- [[Silicon Valley Emergence]]: Silicon Valley emerged from an agricultural area, evolving into a federally funded research and defense economy driven by wartime and Cold War investment.
- [[Stanford Research Park]]: Stanford Research Park is the first university-owned industrial park, created by leasing land that the university was barred from selling.
- [[Venture Capital]]: Venture capital is a form of investment that finances high-risk startups by taking equity and absorbing potential losses, which was initially supported by government programs.
