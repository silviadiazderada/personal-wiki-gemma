# Counterculture Ethos

The counterculture ethos of the 1960s merged with technology, leading to a philosophy of personal liberation through computing.

## Key points
- The Berkeley Free Speech Movement (1964) was part of the Bay Area's counterculture, which distrusted big institutions. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 14)
- Pam Hardt-English led Community Memory, a public computer terminal in a Berkeley record store; Lee Felsenstein was MC of the Homebrew Computer Club. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slides 12, 14)
- The anti-establishment ethos became the self-image of the industry, surviving even as companies became large corporations. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 14)
