# Five Pillars of Silicon Valley

The five pillars that made Silicon Valley competitive over the long term are government policy, research universities, venture capital, immigrants, and the legal system.

## Key points
- The state / government policy War and great-power competition have driven US innovation for 80 years. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 5)
- In 1958, ~80% of Fairchild's business was government contracts. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 5)
- Stanford held a large endowment of land it was barred from selling; Fred Terman's answer was to lease it — creating Stanford Research Park (1951), the first university-owned industrial park. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 8)
- Valley law firms took equity instead of fees and built the scaffolding — incorporation, term sheets, M&A, IPOs. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 5)
- California won't enforce non-competes (full ban since 2024; still enforceable in New York) → engineers job-hop → ideas and talent cross-pollinate → more dynamic than Boston's Route 128. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 15)
