# Venture Capital

Venture capital is a funding mechanism that financed un-financeable ventures by putting in equity and absorbing losses, though it has become an insular network.

## Key points
- Venture capital put in equity, took a board seat, and absorbed the loss when a company failed — which made funding high-risk ventures possible at all. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 13)
- The 1958 Small Business Investment Act created the Small Business Investment Company program: the federal government licensed private investment funds and leveraged them, lending roughly $2–$3 of low-cost government money for every $1 of private capital. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 13)
- Venture capital is still an insular “old-boys network.” Many partners are former founders who back people like themselves; talent is evenly distributed, opportunity is not. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 13)
