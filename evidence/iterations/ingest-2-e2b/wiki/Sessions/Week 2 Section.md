# Week 2 Section

This source reviews the history of Silicon Valley, tracing its emergence from an agricultural region to a federally funded research economy. It examines the five pillars that shaped the Valley, including government policy, research universities, venture capital, immigration, and the legal system, while also exploring the counterculture thread and structural inequalities.

## Topics covered
- [[Counterculture Thread]]: The 1960s Bay Area was the center of the American counterculture, which fused with the defense industry to create a paradox where anti-establishment ethos became the marketing for powerful industries.
- [[Five Pillars of Silicon Valley]]: The five pillars that made Silicon Valley competitive over the long term are government policy, research universities, venture capital, immigrants, and the legal system.
- [[Fred Terman]]: Fred Terman is known as the 'Father of Silicon Valley' for bringing the MIT military/industry model to Stanford and building the Research Park.
- [[Immigrants in Tech]]: Immigrants have become a significant part of the tech workforce, with a growing share in high-tech firms and a role in talent pipelines.
- [[Stanford Research Park]]: Stanford Research Park was created by Fred Terman to lease land it could not sell, creating a working loop between technology firms and the university.
- [[Vannevar Bush]]: Vannevar Bush was a key figure in shaping federal science funding by proposing the concept of 'Science, the Endless Frontier'.
- [[Venture Capital]]: Venture capital is a funding mechanism that financed un-financeable ventures by putting in equity and absorbing losses, though it has become an insular network.
