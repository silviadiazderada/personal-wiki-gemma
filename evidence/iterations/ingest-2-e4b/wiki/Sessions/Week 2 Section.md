# Week 2 Section

This source provides a comprehensive review of the historical development of Silicon Valley, detailing the five key pillars that fostered its growth. It covers the role of government funding, research universities, venture capital, immigrant populations, and the legal framework, while also touching upon the counterculture's influence.

## Topics covered
- [[Federal Funding]]: Federal and military money has been a primary driver of US innovation for decades, forming the foundation of the tech economy.
- [[Five Pillars]]: The Five Pillars are the five key factors that shaped and maintain Silicon Valley's competitive edge over the long term.
- [[Fred Terman]]: Fred Terman is recognized as the 'Father of Silicon Valley' for his efforts to tie Stanford to defense work and industry.
- [[Immigrant Founders]]: Immigrants play a significant role in Silicon Valley, with a substantial percentage of high-tech firms having immigrant founders.
- [[Legal System]]: The Valley's legal system supports innovation by allowing firms to take equity instead of fees and by not enforcing non-compete agreements.
- [[Stanford Research Park]]: Stanford Research Park is the first university-owned industrial park, created by leasing land that Stanford was barred from selling.
- [[Venture Capital]]: Venture capital is a form of funding that allows high-risk ventures to survive by taking equity and absorbing potential losses.
