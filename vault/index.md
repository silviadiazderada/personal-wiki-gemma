## Concepts

- [[Counterculture Ethos]]: The counterculture ethos of the 1960s merged with technology, leading to a philosophy of personal liberation through computing.
- [[Five Pillars of Silicon Valley]]: The five pillars of Silicon Valley (the state, research universities, venture capital, immigrants and the legal system) are the factors that have shaped and maintained Silicon Valley's competitive edge over the long term.
- [[Immigrant Founders]]: Immigrant founders are one of the five pillars of Silicon Valley: immigrants come for education, often through Berkeley and Stanford, and a large share go on to found companies.
- [[Legal System]]: California's legal framework supports tech innovation through Valley law firms that take equity instead of fees and through California's refusal to enforce non-compete agreements (a full ban since 2024).
- [[Origins of Silicon Valley]]: Silicon Valley emerged from an agricultural area, evolving into a federally funded research and defense economy driven by wartime and Cold War investment.
- [[Silicon Valley vs Route 128]]: Silicon Valley vs Route 128 is AnnaLee Saxenian's comparison of two tech regions: the open, networked Valley and Boston's hierarchical, insular Route 128.
- [[Startup Discovery Platforms]]: Several platforms are provided to help students research and identify potential startups for the competition.
- [[Venture Capital]]: Venture capital is a form of investment that finances high-risk startups by taking equity and absorbing potential losses, which was initially supported by government programs.

## People

- [[Fred Terman]]: Fred Terman is recognized as the 'Father of Silicon Valley' for establishing the model of linking academia with industry at Stanford.
- [[Janet Yellen]]: Janet Yellen is a prominent economist whose career reflects a focus on the state's role in economic security and employment.
- [[Jeff Bezos]]: Jeff Bezos appears in the Week 4 reflection, which asks what 'thorny problem' early online retail faced and how Bezos solved it, according to O'Mara.
- [[Sergey Brin and Larry Page]]: Sergey Brin and Larry Page created a search engine; the Week 4 reflection asks, from O'Mara's The Code, what motivated them and what role the federal government played early on.
- [[Silicon Valley Pioneers]]: Silicon Valley's pioneers are the people the course credits with building the region's research, semiconductor, financing and hobbyist foundations.
- [[Vannevar Bush]]: Vannevar Bush was instrumental in establishing permanent federal funding for scientific research in the United States.

## Organizations

- [[Pitchbook]]: Pitchbook is a database used for investment screening, requiring specific sign-up procedures and adhering to usage limits.
- [[Stanford Research Park]]: Stanford Research Park is the first university-owned industrial park, created by leasing land that Stanford was barred from selling.

## Policies and Events

- [[Government Funding]]: Government funding is the course's 'state' theme: federal money built the foundational technology of Silicon Valley, from DARPA and ARPANET to GPS.
- [[Non-Compete Agreements]]: Non-compete agreements are not enforced in California (a full ban since 2024), which keeps engineers moving between firms.
- [[SBIC Program]]: The Small Business Investment Company (SBIC) program was a federal initiative that leveraged private capital to fund early high-risk ventures.

## Course Logistics

- [[Founder Interview]]: The founder interview is a graded checkpoint where the entire team participates in a 30–60 minute discussion with the startup's founder or a tech professional.
- [[Founder Outreach]]: Effective outreach to busy founders requires a professional, targeted approach, and students must begin this process immediately.
- [[Midterm Exam]]: The Midterm Exam is scheduled for Thursday 10/1 during lecture and will be open note and open book.
- [[Midterm Themes]]: The course explores several major themes related to Silicon Valley, including immigration, the role of the state, and regional models.
- [[Professional Profile Building]]: Building a professional profile involves taking proactive steps to gain experience and demonstrate capability.
- [[Startup Selection Rules]]: Startups chosen for the competition must meet specific criteria, including being pre-Series A, founded in Silicon Valley, and no more than three years old.
- [[Team Start-Up Competition]]: The Team Start-Up Competition requires students to adopt a real early-stage startup, interview its founder, and pitch it at Demo Day, accounting for 20% of the course grade.
