# Origins of Silicon Valley

Silicon Valley emerged from an agricultural area, evolving into a federally funded research and defense economy driven by wartime and Cold War investment.

## Key points
- Before the war, the area was known as the “Valley of Heart's Delight” and featured a small radio-and-electronics cluster. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 6)
- World War II saw Vannevar Bush's Office of Scientific Research and Development pour federal money into university labs. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 6)
- Sputnik (1957) triggered a surge in funding through NASA, ARPA, and the National Defense Education Act (1958). ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 6)
- The ecosystem led to the spin-out of semiconductors: Shockley (1956) → Fairchild (1957) → Intel (1968). ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 6)

## Related notes
- [[Stanford Research Park]]: Stanford Research Park was the institutional mechanism of the origin story: federal military money channeled through a research university into private firms.
