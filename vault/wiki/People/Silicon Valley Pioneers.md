# Silicon Valley Pioneers

Silicon Valley's pioneers are the people the course credits with building the region's research, semiconductor, financing and hobbyist foundations.

## Key points
- [[Vannevar Bush]] ran WWII federal R&D and advocated for permanent federal science funding with 'Science, the Endless Frontier' (1945). ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 12)
- [[Fred Terman]] is known as the 'Father of Silicon Valley' for bringing the MIT military/industry model to Stanford. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 12)
- William Shockley co-invented the transistor (Nobel Prize, 1956) and founded Shockley Semiconductor (1956), which brought silicon to the Valley. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 12)
- Arthur Rock financed the 'Traitorous Eight' and made 'venture capitalist' a job title by funding and chairing Intel. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 12)
- Lee Felsenstein (Cal EECS) was MC of the Homebrew Computer Club and designed the Osborne 1, the first mass-produced portable. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 12)
- Pam Hardt-English, a Cal CS dropout, led Community Memory, a public computer terminal in a Berkeley record store. ([[raw/POLECON156_Week2_DiscussionSection.pptx|Week 2 Section]], slide 12)

## Related notes
